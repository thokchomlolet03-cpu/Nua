# Initial synthetic teacher-agent review

Synthetic lower-secondary science educator simulation for ages 13–14. This is not a qualified human review and does not establish educational validation.

## Scope and constraints

- Initial review source: `lesson.json` only. No student fixtures, application code, external research or learner-response records were inspected.
- Findings are anticipated issues, not conclusions supported by learner performance or completion-time evidence.
- All 20+ distinct inquiry types remain REQUIRED. The supplied baseline contains q1–q20; none is proposed for removal or optional status.
- The baseline lesson remains frozen for this run. Corrections below are proposals for a later revision, not implementation instructions for this run.
- Actual application-produced synthetic response records are pending. Later feedback should link findings to those records and retain their synthetic evidence status.

## Prioritized findings

### 1. Clarify hypothetical results and claims — q10, q13, q20

q20 refers to “this fertilizer result,” although no results are supplied. q13 leaves unclear whether the imagined plant belongs to the tested group and does not state the claim being challenged. q10 leaves the predicted effect’s direction open.

Example corrections for a later revision:

- **q20:** “Suppose a completed fair comparison found greater growth with fertilizer. Ask whether that finding would apply in another setting, and describe the evidence needed.”
- **q13:** “Suppose fertilizer increased average growth in the tested group. A plant of another species showed no increase. Does that contradict the claim about the tested group? Explain.”
- **q10:** “Suppose fertilizer increases average growth under these conditions. What pattern would you expect across repeated comparisons? Could individual measurements differ?”

### 2. Differentiate every required inquiry type and manage burden — q1–q20

**Rejected suggestion:** The initial review proposed q1, q2, q8, q10, q12, q15, q18 and q19 as an eight-question core, with other questions used selectively. The founder rejected this recommendation. It is not the proposed direction: all 20+ distinct inquiry types stay required.

Different labels alone do not establish distinct reasoning. q1, q3, q7, q9, q11, q14 and q17 repeatedly address alternative explanations; q12, q13 and q20 repeatedly address limits on generalization. Twenty open responses to one short paragraph may produce repetition and fatigue, but actual burden remains unmeasured.

Instead, differentiate the required outputs in a future revision:

| Question | Distinct reasoning and observable output |
| --- | --- |
| q1 — definition | State the defining conditions of a fair fertilizer test and distinguish a non-example. |
| q2 — reconstruction | Reconstruct an ordered procedure, including what changes, controls, measurement and repetition. |
| q3 — mechanism | Explain how equal conditions reduce alternative explanations; distinguish this design logic from an absent biological explanation. |
| q4 — evidence | Separate a source-supported design claim from an empirical claim requiring growth data. |
| q5 — assumptions | Identify a comparability assumption, a possible failure and its consequence. |
| q6 — prerequisites | Explain “variable” through a changed condition and a measured outcome before applying role labels. |
| q7 — comparison | Compare two explicit designs side by side and identify the consequential difference. |
| q8 — classification | Assign light, water, fertilizer and growth to roles, with a reason for each assignment. |
| q9 — causality | Judge whether a stated causal claim follows from a design and justify the judgment. |
| q10 — prediction | Predict a conditional pattern before seeing results, allowing variation. |
| q11 — counterfactual | Change only water equality in the proposed design and trace how that changes interpretation. |
| q12 — boundaries | State the tested scope and identify a specific extension requiring further evidence. |
| q13 — counterexample | Decide whether a hypothetical case contradicts an explicitly bounded claim. |
| q14 — error | Locate the faulty inference in a supplied conclusion and rewrite it accurately. |
| q15 — uncertainty | Identify missing information and explain which judgment each missing item prevents. |
| q16 — representation | Design table headings with units and repetition identifiers; explain what the table alone cannot establish. |
| q17 — interpretation | Generate alternative explanations and propose a comparison that helps distinguish them. |
| q18 — application | Map the principle to a sufficiently described new investigation with a measurable outcome. |
| q19 — synthesis | Explain the distinct, complementary contributions of controls and repetition. |
| q20 — inquiry | Formulate a new, answerable question and an evidence plan specifying conditions and measurements. |

Provide richer material in a future version: two explicit design descriptions, an operational definition of growth and measurement duration, a clearly labeled synthetic repeated-measurement table, contrasting bounded and universal claims, and a short paper-helicopter description. Keep design-only questions before any results reveal. These additions are proposals; the current frozen source supplies no growth measurements.

A provisional pacing option retains every question across three sessions: q1–q8 for foundations and design; q9–q16 for inference, uncertainty and representation; q17–q20 for interpretation, application, synthesis and inquiry. Use short classifications, tables and focused explanations where appropriate instead of requiring an essay for every item. Session allocation and burden require later evidence; they are not validated timings.

### 3. Supply missing scaffolding — q3, q6, q18

q3’s biological-mechanism question is underspecified because several biological processes are absent. q6 asks an abstract question about prerequisites without defining “variable.” q18 assumes familiarity with paper helicopters and gives no investigation aim.

Example corrections for a later revision:

- **q3:** “Explain how keeping light and water equal helps test fertilizer’s effect. Does the passage also explain how fertilizer changes plant growth?”
- **q6:** “A variable is something that can change. Identify what is deliberately changed and what is measured. How does this distinction help you describe the test?”
- **q18:** “A paper helicopter spins as it falls. Investigate whether blade length affects fall time. What would you change, keep equal and measure?”

### 4. Make success criteria directly observable — q5, q10, q12, q15, q17, q20

Phrases such as “relevant details,” “plausible alternatives” and “evidence plan” leave acceptance thresholds unclear. Some criteria require qualifications that the prompt does not explicitly request. Make expected evidence visible in both the prompt and criterion while accepting equivalent wording.

Example **q15 criterion:** “Requests growth measurements for both fertilizer conditions over the same duration, repeated measurements to examine variation, and confirmation that light and water were equal. Does not assert an observed effect.”

For **q17**, accept light and fertilizer as possible contributors without requiring students to treat them as mutually exclusive. For **q20**, require a named changed setting, a testable question, a relevant comparison and a measured outcome; do not require an invented result.

### 5. Repair evidence anchors — q1–q20

Every question has the same anchor, truncated mid-word at “fertili.” It omits the full procedure and scope limitation needed particularly for q2, q8, q12, q13, q19 and q20. The full page does contain those statements, so this is an anchor-specific deficiency, not their absence from the entire source.

In a later revision, use complete, question-specific source sentences. For q18, identify the source principle separately from the new application context.

## Features to preserve

q4 and q15 distinguish design descriptions from observed data. q8 provides a concrete classification task. q19 separates controlling conditions from observing variation. Preserve these distinctions while improving differentiation across all required inquiry types.
