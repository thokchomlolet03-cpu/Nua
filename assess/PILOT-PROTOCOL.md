# Mangal Inquiry — pilot and evaluation protocol draft

Version 1, 13 September 2026. Proposed, not an executed study or an ethics approval. No learner recruitment, data collection, partner agreement or external sharing is authorized by this document.

## Purpose and initial boundary

Determine whether a reviewed 20-plus-angle inquiry produces interpretable evidence that educators can use without excessive authoring or review time. Proposed starting audience: learners aged 12–16 studying one science unit in English, supervised by an educator. Confirm this with the founder and intended school before recruitment.

Keep the core constant: at least 20 genuinely distinct question types tied to one objective. Divide the set across manageable sessions. Do not infer that finishing more questions establishes mastery or that the number 20 has been scientifically validated as optimal.

## Phase A — educator review without learner data

Proposed feasibility sample: 2–3 educators and 5–10 short, permitted lessons. These numbers are planning suggestions, not a statistical power calculation.

For each lesson:

1. Record the objective, passage, grade context, prerequisites and excluded diagrams or concepts.
2. Generate a template plan. Optionally compare a local-model draft of the same requested types; do not change model settings mid-comparison without recording the change.
3. Have reviewers assess every question before assignment. Keep original drafts and an external review worksheet so revisions are not confused with generation quality.
4. Record question creation/revision time with an observer or timer. Application timestamps are device times, not active work duration.
5. Resolve disagreement, correct the plan and export the reviewed lesson. Re-import on another device and verify that review flags reset and learner attempts do not transfer.

Review worksheet fields: lesson ID; question ID/type; draft origin/model/version; source sufficient (yes/no); factual problem (description); objective relevance (0–2); distinct reasoning (0–2); age/language clarity (0–2); observable criterion (0–2); accept/edit/reject; required edit; review minutes.

Common anchors for each 0–2 rating: 0 = absent or unsuitable; 1 = partially present and needs substantive revision; 2 = adequate for this lesson and intended audience. Reviewers should add dimension-specific examples before use. These ratings describe item quality, not learner ability.

Suggested operational gate, to agree before reviewing results: no unresolved factual/source-sufficiency defect, all required types educationally defensible, and teacher workload within a teacher-agreed budget. Do not approve filler to satisfy the count. Expand material or reconsider the objective if necessary.

## Phase B — supervised workflow feasibility

Begin only after the school/research lead establishes appropriate permission, consent/assent where required, safeguarding, retention, deletion and access procedures. Do not put names, contact details or sensitive personal narratives in responses. Pseudonyms can still be identifying; local storage is not a secure institutional records service.

Observe a small agreed learner group across the complete sequence:

material preparation → initial recall → all 20+ inquiry responses across sessions → learner question and synthesis/revision → delayed application → educator next step → learner follow-up.

If using the immediate demo, retain its label and exclude it from delayed-retention analyses. Observe rather than infer off-screen help. If a learner needs help or becomes fatigued, prioritize support and record the changed condition; do not pressure completion to protect the study design.

The current app only opens educator feedback after application. If the classroom needs intervention earlier, record that requirement and the assistance provided. Do not describe post-application review as real-time teacher monitoring. A future mode would need explicit evidence-condition changes before implementation.

Educator review procedure:

1. Examine a flagged or otherwise useful response and the question's criterion/source.
2. Quote the exact part supporting the interpretation. Exact matching is a provenance check, not proof that the interpretation is correct.
3. Choose needs clarification, partly supported, or supported by this response. Do not infer a global skill score.
4. Specify one teaching/practice action and what successful subsequent reasoning would show.
5. Ask the learner to try it and save a follow-up. A human checks the follow-up against the stated criterion; the app does not automatically judge success.

## Measures and interpretation limits

| Question | Measure | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Are drafts usable? | Accepted without substantive edit / all draft questions; edit/reject reasons | Report per lesson and generation method; do not hide rejected drafts |
| Is preparation practical? | Observed authoring/review minutes per reusable lesson | Device timestamps include inactivity and cannot substitute for observed time |
| Is breadth meaningful? | Independent distinctness ratings and examples of duplicated reasoning | Twenty labels alone do not establish twenty cognitive demands |
| Is pacing feasible? | Questions/session, pauses, completion, learner-reported fatigue | Completion is not mastery; missingness may reflect access or burden |
| Can teachers act? | Review minutes; specific evidence-to-action links; educator-rated usefulness | Software quotation validation cannot establish instructional validity |
| Is help visible? | Optional unsure/help signals and subsequent review | Missing signal is not confidence; signals are self-report, not diagnosis |
| Does follow-up meet its criterion? | Independent human assessment using pre-agreed criterion | It occurs after feedback and is not an independent pre/post comparison |
| Does reuse work? | Import/review success, offline completion, device failures, support time | A desktop check does not establish low-end phone performance |

For double-rated responses, report raw agreement and disagreements with examples. A researcher should select an appropriate reliability statistic and study size; do not select a flattering statistic after observing results. Any later efficacy study needs a defensible comparison, matched conditions, analysis of missing data and sufficient sample size. A small convenience pilot cannot establish causal learning gains.

## Data handling and research contribution

Current records include question/type IDs, draft origins, criteria/source anchors, response text, support conditions, learner self-reports, device timestamps, interpretation/quotation/next-step/check, and separately linked follow-up. Summary export omits raw material and response/feedback prose; full export includes them. Neither export is automatically anonymous. Educator identity is not authenticated. External assistance and clock accuracy are not verified.

Keep a consent/access key outside the app if the institution approves such a procedure. Establish who may hold files, permitted devices, retention period and a deletion process before collection. Do not send full exports to cloud models or publish them by default. Reporting publicly should use approved aggregates and synthetic examples; material copyright and learner privacy are separate checks.

Candidate public goods: versioned taxonomy, this review rubric/protocol, schema documentation and synthetic evaluation fixtures. Publication and license selection remain separate decisions. Record limitations and negative findings as well as successes.

## Decision at the end

Proceed only if educators can prepare credible sets, learners can use the complete sequence with manageable pacing, and the resulting evidence changes a specific instructional action. If quality fails, improve source selection and authoring before adding subjects. If workload fails, improve reuse and review ergonomics without removing the agreed inquiry breadth. If follow-ups do not provide interpretable evidence, revise the criteria and teaching step before automating scoring.
